02 / MOBILE UX · INTERACTION DESIGN

Less effort between
need and care.

A mobile-first concept for booking a routine campus health appointment. The flow makes the choices visible, validates contact details clearly, and ends with a calm confirmation.

01 / THE SCENARIO

Make the next step obvious.

A student needs to book a routine appointment between classes. They want to see who they’ll meet, compare available times, and understand what happens after submitting their details.

The concept treats booking as a short, linear task. Provider and appointment type are stated up front; date and time are easy to compare; contact fields appear before the final confirmation.

Scope note: This is a fictional campus clinic concept. The provider, service, and appointment data are sample content. The prototype does not transmit or store form data, and it is not a real booking service or medical advice.

02 / USER JOURNEY

Choose → review → confirm.

  1. Review the service and provider.
  2. Choose a day from the available dates.
  3. Select an available appointment time.
  4. Enter contact information and check the appointment summary.
  5. Submit to see an on-screen confirmation.

The prototype intentionally keeps this first version to one screen so visitors can understand the whole flow before interacting. The sequence is reflected in a four-part progress indicator.

03 / INTERACTION CHOICES

Make selection and recovery feel simple.

  • Use buttons for date and time choices so selection works with mouse, keyboard, and touch.
  • Keep the chosen date and time in a visible summary before confirmation.
  • Use field-level error messages and focus the first invalid field instead of showing a vague form error.
  • After a valid submit, show a confirmation with the selected details and a clear way to start again.

Only the front-end flow is demonstrated. There is no calendar integration, account login, insurance collection, or backend reservation.

04 / WHAT I’D TEST NEXT

Test clarity, confidence, and error recovery.

I’d ask people to book a routine visit on a phone-sized screen, then observe whether they understand the date options, know which time is selected, and can recover from a missing or mistyped email. I’d also ask what they expect to happen after confirmation.

No bookings, health information, or contact details are sent by this prototype. No usability results are claimed.
